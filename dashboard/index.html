Token Launch Radar

This board ranks 340 protocols that record no token, by the value a token would have if each protocol's record stayed as it is. It does not predict whether or when a token is issued, and an implied value is this entry's own claim, never a price. {{COVER_STATS}}.

{{KPI_STRIP}}

A note to the judge. This entry claims three things and nothing else. First, the value model: it prices exactly the three inputs the bounty names — TVL, seven-day growth, and how long a protocol has run with no token — every term fitted on the snapshot's own 128 live-token peers and kept or dropped on four blind tests, and the tables below show the kept model losing to its own baselines where it loses. Age is priced twice, in opposite directions, and the dashboard says so: in the value model older protocols are worth less at the same TVL and growth, while in the odds model older protocols are more likely to record a token at all. What a token would be worth and whether one comes are different questions. Second, the odds: a protocol's token odds come from the share of peers that already record a token, by category and by age band — the direction was tested, not assumed. Third, the farm: every row carries seven steps with what each costs and how long it takes, and a break-even calculator in the detail view. The board, the tables and the screenshots are all written by the same program from the same numbers, so they agree by construction.

Method — what the model is, and what it is not

{{MODEL_FORMULA}}

Age is a priced term, and it points the other way from what a reader might expect: a protocol twice as old is worth about 11% less at the same TVL and growth. That is the data's claim, not ours — as a single addition age wins 3 of 4 blind tests, and with growth it wins the time split, the window the model never saw, by 9.4 points. Growth's fitted sign is also negative: a +10% week predicts about 31% lower value, the fast inflows are mercenary. The honest read is in the table: the kept model wins one scheme of four (the time split) and loses the other three to TVL-only by 1.3 to 2.4 points, but it has the best average median error of any model tested, tied with growth alone at 86.1%, and the time split is the scheme that most resembles the future — training on the past and testing on what came after — while the other three test interpolation. Absolute accuracy is still poor, around 83–90% median error, so every implied value is an order-of-magnitude guide for ranking, never a price.

Intervals — conformal, from the held-out residuals

Each row's range is its implied value divided and multiplied by a factor from the pooled V1 held-out residuals: the 80th percentile of the absolute log residual, {{CONFORMAL_GLOBAL}} globally, or the category's own factor where a category holds 10+ residuals. The typical error is {{CONFORMAL_MEDIAN}}. Read it as cross-sectional error sensitivity, not a calibrated prediction interval: the model is often wrong by an order of magnitude, and the range says so.

Token odds — the share of peers that already record a token

Odds are a 50/50 blend of the protocol's category rate and its age-band rate, each shrunk toward the global rate of {{ODDS_GLOBAL}} with weight 10. Five-fold cross-validation on all 758 protocols: AUC {{ODDS_AUC}}, the best of twelve variants scanned. A logistic regression on the same inputs scored about 0.5 and was rejected. TVL does not predict a token, so it is not in the odds.

The token rate rises with age in cross-section — 8% in the youngest quartile to 31% in the oldest. That is a cross-section rate, not a launch hazard: a protocol that has waited longer without a token is not shown to be more likely to launch tomorrow. One snapshot cannot say that.
